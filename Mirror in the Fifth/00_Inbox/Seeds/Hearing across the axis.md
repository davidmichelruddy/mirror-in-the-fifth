# Hearing across the axis

- dissonance in the collections (avoid notes) – [[2026-10-31]]
- strongest is P4 played over the M3 (half step or m9?) – [[2026-10-31]]
- in the reciprocal minor this relationship is heard as M7 (the pole anchoring the ear): E♭ against D in C minor is m2 or M7? – [[2026-10-31]]
- this is hearing across the axis – [[2026-10-31]]
- essentially the difference on the axes between sum 0 – (G F♯) vs. (G♭ G) – [[2026-10-31]]
	- same pitch class, arrived at differently in the lattice: the antipode

> [!ai] Questions, flags, links
> - Checked, "P4 over the M3": F over E in C major is ic1, a half step or m9. The mirror under sum 7 sends E→E♭ and F→D, so the minor counterpart is D against E♭, the same ic1 from the other side. That matches your E♭/D jot.
> - The "sum 0" line, read through the antipode: under C's own mirror (sum 0), F♯ (six fifths up) and G♭ (six fifths down) are twins, a Pythagorean comma apart in the lattice and merged in equal temperament. So G–F♯ and G♭–G are one twelve-tone pair (sum 1) but two lattice pairs, one from each arm. Question: does the ear hear the half step pointing differently depending on which arm it came from, i.e. is the antipode audible here, or only notational?
