# Cadence and the balance of sides

- authentic cadence: gathers all the energy of the key in appropriate order – [[2026-10-31]]
- full complement of pitches in the set requires visiting subdominant, dominant and tonic – [[2026-10-31]]
- convincingly establishes key because it fulfills the obligation to present the balance of both sides → leaves the center irrefutable – [[2026-10-31]]
- does the major / bright side have the stronger pull, or is the upward tendency stronger? – [[2026-10-31]]
- is the bright leading tone stronger directionally? – [[2026-10-31]]

> [!ai] Questions, flags, links
> - Checked: the I, IV and V triads together hold all seven diatonic pitches, and none of the three pairs does. So "full complement" is exact for IV–V–I; ii–V–I also covers all seven (ii has D F A).
> - Question: if balance is what establishes the key, why does V–I alone (missing F's side) work so well in practice? Is the dominant seventh's F doing the subdominant's job? That's the "spine F" idea in the Restructured argument's parked list.
> - Question for the bright-pull jot: the ascending leading tone B→C is the bright side's; the descending ♭6→5 (A♭→G) is the dark side's mirror. Is the asymmetry in the ear, or in which of the two is usually harmonized as dominant?
> - Theorists: Rameau (cadence parfaite, the subdominant's place), Hauptmann (key as the union of three triads), Riemann (T–S–D–T as the complete cadence), Harrison, *Harmonic Function in Chromatic Music* (1994), on dualist cadences.
