## Bears on

- **Root-at-bottom, not poles.** Russell's founding rule: "The tonic of an interval of a fifth is its lower tone" (p. 1); a fourth's tonic is its upper tone (p. 5). That's the ear's root-at-bottom fact, which the book keeps separate from the mirror (CLAUDE.md, "Poles, not roots")
- **One direction only.** Gravity "flows in a downward direction" down a stack of fifths, conferring authority on the lowest tone (p. 3). A fifths-based theory with no mirror: a contrast with Mathieu's two directions (major up from C, minor down from G)
- **Lydian as the bright edge.** Lydian = six fifths up from one tone (p. 1); vault has Lydian ↔ Phrygian as a mirror pair and "Lydian keeps its anchor" → [[The Mirror in the Fifth (tab)]] (Windows and modes; grounded edge)
- **Compression thread.** Chord/scale unity (the "chordmode") is the source of jazz chord-scale theory: a chord symbol read as a scale. A candidate example of an improviser's compression; for its spread into jazz textbooks see [[Reading queue]]
- Overtone claim to check: fifth as the "strongest harmonic interval" and basis of tonal gravity (pp. 2–3)
